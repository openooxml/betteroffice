Fit every unsized row to its tallest content, and read an `<xf>` whose `applyX` flag is simply absent as applying its indexed font, fill, border and number format. A row with no `ht` now takes the height Excel gives it — measured off Excel's own renders as `1.4615 × point size − 2.0769`, snapped to Excel's quarter-point grid, which reproduces Calibri 11 at 14.0pt and Comic Sans MS 24 at 33.0pt exactly — while `sheetFormatPr/@customHeight` still pins every unsized row at the declared default and an authored `ht` still wins. A `defaultRowHeight` declared without `customHeight` is the cached hint Excel recomputes, so a row with content there takes its fitted height outright; a sheet that declares none keeps its font-derived default and only taller content grows a row. Together with the `applyX` reading, which follows ECMA-376 §18.8.45 and leaves an explicit `applyX="0"` holding its facet back, a sheet whose 24pt and 30pt headings carry their font through an `applyAlignment`-only `<xf>` now paints those headings in rows tall enough to hold them: sheet content height on the `vietnamese-lunar-calendar` sample moves from 647px to 1010px against Excel's 1013px. Row heights stay a render projection — a fitted height is never written back, so an untouched sheet still re-serializes byte-identically.
